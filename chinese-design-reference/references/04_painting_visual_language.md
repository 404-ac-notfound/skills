# Module 04 — Chinese Painting and Visual Language (山水·花鳥·界畫·詩書畫印)

**Scope.** This module surveys the ink-painting traditions most relevant to designing a Chinese–English family-history book — landscape (山水), flower-and-bird (花鳥), and architectural ruled-line painting (界畫) — and the integrated art of poetry, calligraphy, painting and seals (詩書畫印). It then asks how these principles can inform contemporary design **without** reducing Chinese culture to decorative cliché.

**Claim labels:** (A) documented fact or primary statement, (B) visual observation, (C) scholarly interpretation, (D) proposed contemporary design application. Where (D) appears, it is explicitly *not* historical practice.

---

## 1. The three genres in brief

### 1.1 山水 (mountain-water, landscape)

Landscape is the prestige genre of Chinese painting, conceived not as topography but as a habitable moral space. Guo Xi 郭熙 (ca. 1000–ca. 1090), in the treatise 《林泉高致》 (*Linquan Gaozhi*, "Lofty Ambitions in Forests and Streams", compiled by his son Guo Si), insists a landscape must be "walkable, viewable, livable, wanderable" (可行、可望、可居、可游) — a formulation the National Palace Museum still uses to describe his masterpiece *Early Spring* (A).[^1^] His theory of animation is worth quoting at length because it is a design system in itself (A):[^2^]

> 山以水為血脈，以草木為毛髮，以煙雲為神彩 — "Mountains take water as their blood vessels, plants as their hair, and mist-clouds as their complexion; a mountain without mist is like a spring without flowers and grass."

### 1.2 花鳥 (flower-and-bird)

Flower-and-bird painting (extending to fish, insects, fruits) developed from Song court naturalism — extreme, specimen-like fidelity — toward the literati mode in which plum, orchid, bamboo and chrysanthemum (the 四君子, Four Gentlemen) carry ethical meanings: resilience, purity, uprightness, reclusion (A/C). For a family-history book this genre matters because its plants are *biographical*: orchids for scholarly virtue, pine for endurance of lineage, plum for survival through winter (C/D).

### 1.3 界畫 (ruled-line architectural painting)

Jiehua is the most misunderstood genre. Buildings were drawn with a 界尺 (jiechi): an ungraduated ruler with a groove along which a brush, clamped against a split bamboo guide or a second stick (筆船), could slide to produce perfectly straight, evenly spaced lines — making jiehua, in the Freer Gallery's words, "the only non-freehand style of Chinese painting" (A).[^3^][^4^]

History (A):[^5^][^6^]

- The earliest large surviving jiehua is the **Que Tower mural (《闕樓圖》)** in the Tang tomb of Crown Prince Yide 懿德太子 (d. 701).
- Song catalogues (《宣和畫譜》) classed buildings as 宮室/屋木; the term 界畫 itself arose in the Southern Song (A).[^3^]
- Northern Song master **Guo Zhongshu 郭忠恕** was ranked "divine class" (神品) for jiehua; the Southern Song academy painter **Li Song 李嵩** (e.g. 《水殿招涼圖》) fused precise architecture into poetic landscape (A).[^6^]
- Yuan dynasty revival: **Wang Zhenpeng 王振鵬** (《龍舟圖》/金明池 scrolls), **Li Rongjin 李容瑾**, and the Hangzhou professional **Xia Yong 夏永** (active mid-14th c.), whose album leaves of famous towers — Yueyang Tower (1347), Yellow Crane Tower, Pavilion of Prince Teng — render whole buildings in lines praised as "fine as a mosquito's eyelash, equal to the work of demons" (細若蚊睫，侔於鬼工), sometimes with an entire prose text such as Fan Zhongyan's 《岳陽樓記》 inscribed in fly-head small script beside the image (A).[^3^][^7^]
- Qing: **Yuan Jiang 袁江 and Yuan Yao 袁耀** pushed jiehua away from architectural documentation toward imagined, inhabitable fantasy palaces (A).[^6^]

Scholarship note: jiehua was long dismissed by literati critics precisely because the ruler suppressed visible "brush personality"; recent scholarship (e.g. Leqi Yu, *Painting Architecture: Jiehua in Yuan China, 1271–1368*, HKU Press, 2022) reframes it as a sophisticated practice negotiating documentation, fantasy and commerce (C).[^8^] **Relevance to this project (D):** a Fuzhou village lineage hall, an ancestral home, or a Malaysian shophouse street could be recorded in jiehua's disciplined line as a dignified alternative to nostalgic photo-collage.

## 2. Landscape composition: the Three Distances (三遠法)

Guo Xi's *Linquan Gaozhi* states (A):[^2^]

> 山有三遠：自山下而仰山巔，謂之高遠；自山前而窺山後，謂之深遠；自近山而望遠山，謂之平遠。
> "Mountains have three distances: gazing up at the summit from the mountain's foot is *high distance*; peering from the mountain's front to what lies behind it is *deep distance*; looking from a near mountain toward distant mountains is *level distance*."

He attaches optical behaviour to each (A):[^2^]

| Distance | Viewpoint | Colour/ink behaviour (Guo Xi's own words) | Compositional character | Later exemplar |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 高遠 High distance | Looking up | 色清明 — clear, bright tones; 勢突兀 — abrupt, towering forms | Vertical, awe; monumental Northern Song hanging scroll | Guo Xi, *Early Spring* 早春圖 (1072), NPM Taipei [^1^] |
| 深遠 Deep distance | Looking through to behind | 色重晦 — heavy, darkened tones; 意重疊 — layered recession | Successive overlapping veils of valley and mist | Layers inside *A Thousand Li of Rivers and Mountains* [^9^] |
| 平遠 Level distance | Near-to-far across | 有明有晦 — mixed light and dark; 意沖融而縹縹緲緲 — blended, drifting | Horizontal, contemplative; low horizon | Guo Xi, *Old Trees, Level Distance* 樹色平遠圖 (ca. 1080), Met 1981.276 [^10^] |

The NPM notes that *Early Spring* deliberately **combines all three distances** in a single hanging scroll — main axis centred, foreground boulders and pines, an S-shaped chain of mid-ground rocks, mist, then twin peaks behind cloud — "presenting an ideal landscape that can be walked, beheld, dwelt in and wandered" (A).[^1^] Later theorists added further distances (Han Zhuo's 韓拙 闊遠/迷遠/幽遠), which are widely cited in secondary literature but were not verified against a primary source in this research — treat as (C, unverified).

**Design translation (D):** the Three Distances are three *camera* strategies plus an atmosphere model. High distance ≈ low-angle hero shot; deep distance ≈ layered depth with occluding scrims; level distance ≈ panoramic calm. Chinese practice adds the crucial extra rule: these are *walked through in sequence* in a handscroll, not held in one frame — composition as itinerary.

### 2.1 The handscroll as sequential composition

The Palace Museum Beijing's description of **Wang Ximeng 王希孟, *A Thousand Li of Rivers and Mountains* (千里江山圖, 1113; silk, colour, 51.5 × 1191.5 cm)** is explicit: the scroll divides into roughly six mountain-centred sections joined by bridges and water so that "each section is relatively independent yet mutually linked… achieving the artistic effect of scenery changing as you move" (步移景異), with the Three Distances interleaved to create rhythm (A).[^9^] The artist was about eighteen and had been personally coached by Emperor Huizong; the scroll is his only surviving work (A).[^11^] Likewise Huang Gongwang's 黃公望 *Dwelling in the Fuchun Mountains* (富春山居圖, begun 1347, completed ca. 1350; paper, ink, 33 × 636.9 cm for the surviving "Master Wuyong" half in Taipei) unrolls as a journey along the Fuchun River in early autumn; the NPM notes the scroll was made over three to four years for his Daoist companion Wuyong (A).[^12^] The scroll was nearly burnt as burial goods in 1650 and survives in two pieces: 《剩山圖》 (Zhejiang Provincial Museum) and 《無用師卷》 (NPM Taipei) (A).[^12^][^13^]

## 3. Texture strokes (皴法): a grammar of rock and earth

Cun 皴 are codified stroke *families* for rendering the grain of mountains and tree bark — abstractions of geology, not shading (A).[^14^] A Republican-era painting manual preserved on Wikimedia Commons gives a workable taxonomy with attributions (A/C):[^14^]

| Cun | Rendering | Associated with | Geology evoked |
|---|---|---|---|
| 披麻皴 hemp-fibre | Long, supple parallel lines like draped hemp | Dong Yuan 董源 (10th c.); Juran; most Yuan masters — Huang Gongwang's *Fuchun Mountains* is its summit (A)[^13^] | Soft earthen Jiangnan hills |
| 斧劈皴 axe-cut | Slashing side-brush facets | Li Sixun (small axe-cut); Li Tang (large); Ma Yuan, Xia Gui | Hard, angular cliff rock |
| 雨點皴 / 豆瓣皴 raindrop/bean | Massed short dots | Fan Kuan (《溪山行旅圖》 NPM) | Weathered granite mass |
| 卷雲皴 curling-cloud | Rotating, inward-curling strokes | Guo Xi (his invention, per tradition) (A)[^2^] | Wind-eroded boulders |
| 米點皴 Mi dots | Wet horizontal blobs | Mi Fu, Mi Youren (拖泥帶水 relatives) | Mist-soaked southern ranges |
| 解索皴 / 牛毛皴 unravelled-rope / ox-hair | Dense, tangled fine lines | Wang Meng 王蒙 | Overgrown, turbulent massifs |
| 折帶皴 folded-ribbon | Flat angular bends | Ni Zan 倪瓚 | Stratified, sparse riverbanks |
| 荷葉皴 lotus-leaf | Veins radiating downhill | Zhao Mengfu (trad.) | Water-eroded ridges |

The same manual adds the key methodological point: the two great families are axe-cut and hemp-fibre; all others are variations, and painters routinely invented personal cun "as the occasion of the mountain required" — the taxonomy is descriptive, not prescriptive (C).[^14^]

**Design translation (D):** cun are *procedural textures with semantic meaning*. Choosing a texture stroke family is like choosing a rendering engine whose parameters encode a landscape type and a lineage. Using a generic "ink brush Photoshop preset" strips out exactly the information (geology, school, period) that makes a texture legible to a trained reader.

## 4. Atmosphere: 雲煙 and 留白

Guo Xi again: "a mountain without mist and cloud is not beautiful; without water it has no allure" (山無雲則不秀，無水則不媚) (A).[^2^] Mist in Song painting is not weather-reporting: it is the *device that negotiates between the distances*, veiling the joins between high, deep and level so the eye travels (C). The Met's discussion of Guo Xi's *Old Trees, Level Distance* quotes his own technique note: "After the outlines are made clear by dark ink strokes, use ink wash mixed with blue to retrace these outlines repeatedly so that… they appear always as if they had just come out of the mist and dew" (A).[^15^]

留白 (reserved white) is the silk or paper left untouched, read as water, sky, mist, snow — the painted world floats on a ground that is materially empty but semantically full (A/C). This is the painterly cognate of calligraphy's 計白當黑 (see Module 03): in *Fuchun Mountains*, broad unworked paper *is* the river (B).[^12^] A design system that borrows 留白 must therefore budget white as a **substance** (water/air/light), not as "empty margin to be filled" (D).

## 5. The integrated work: 詩書畫印 and the 三絕/四全 ideal

### 5.1 Documented development

- Pre-Song paintings rarely carry inscriptions on the image. With the rise of literati painting in the Northern Song, poet-calligrapher-painters such as Su Shi 蘇軾 and Mi Fu 米芾 began writing poems and notes directly on their paintings, and Su's dictum on Wang Wei — "in his poems there are paintings; in his paintings, poems" (詩中有畫，畫中有詩) — became the charter of the ideal (A).[^16^]
- In the Yuan dynasty the seal formally joined the ensemble: Zhao Mengfu 趙孟頫 and Wang冕 (Wang Mian) exemplify the self-conscious unification of the four arts into the "four consummations in one" (四絕一體) (A).[^16^] Zhao's own couplet states the kinship of brush arts: "Rocks [drawn] like flying-white [script], trees like seal script; to write bamboo you must master the Eight Principles — for those who understand, calligraphy and painting are fundamentally one" (石如飛白木如籀，寫竹還應八法通…書畫本來同) (A).[^16^]
- Ming–Qing literati culture made 詩書畫印 the normative frame of both making and judging painting; in the 20th century Wu Changshuo, Qi Baishi, Huang Binhong and Pan Tianshou carried the synthesis to new peaks (A).[^16^]
- The older honorific 三絕 ("Three Perfections" — poetry, calligraphy, painting) is traditionally traced to Tang precedents and was later expanded to 四全/四絕 with seal carving; the exact origin anecdote varies between sources and was not verified against a primary text here (C, partially unverified).

### 5.2 How the four interact on one surface

| Element | Role in the composition (A/C)[^16^] |
|---|---|
| 詩 Poetry | Sets the scene's time, season and moral weather; extends the image beyond what is shown |
| 書 Calligraphy | The inscription is drawn with the same brush logic as the painting — formally continuous, not a caption pasted on |
| 畫 Painting | Supplies the visible world the poem alludes to |
| 印 Seals | Vermilion accents that *count compositionally*: artist's name seal, leisure seals (閒章) with mottos, and collectors' seals (鑑藏印) accreting over centuries |

Collector seals and colophons are not decoration but **accumulated provenance made visible**: Wang Xizhi's *Timely Clearing after Snowfall* (快雪時晴帖, NPM) carries over seventy inscriptions and poems added by the Qianlong emperor, who housed it with two other treasures in the Hall of Three Rarities (三希堂) (A).[^17^] The red seals change the work's colour balance and rhythm — in effect, each generation re-composed the piece (C).

### 5.3 Implications for a family-history book (D)

- A page can be designed as a *four-voice chord*: archival photograph or drawing (畫), a documented family text or poem (詩), calligraphic titling (書), and a seal-like device (印) — but each voice must carry real content. A red square with no documented name or motto is a cliché, not a seal.
- Colophons suggest a respectful layout pattern: later generations comment *around* the ancestral document rather than overwriting it — visually, margin notes in a lighter voice orbiting the primary artefact.

## 6. Using these principles today without cliché

**Documented practice (A) vs. modern interpretation (C) vs. design proposal (D) — keep them separate in your working notes.** Frequent failure modes and their correctives (D):

1. **Ink-wash wallpaper.** Spraying generic splatter behind text borrows nothing but mood. Corrective: commit to *one* historically grounded device per spread — e.g. level-distance layering for a landscape of Qi'an village — and know its name and source.
2. **The decorative red chop.** As above: seals are signatures, mottos and provenance. If the family has no carved seal, commission or design one with a real inscription (e.g. the generation poem 字輩詩), documented as a *new* object of 2026, not a faux-antique.
3. **Random "Chinese" colour.** 青綠山水 blue-green (as in *A Thousand Li of Rivers and Mountains*), ink monochrome, and light colour (淺絳, as in *Fuchun Mountains*) are distinct historical palettes with distinct associations; choose deliberately and say why (A/D).[^9^][^12^]
4. **Flattened perspective jokes.** Chinese painting does not "lack perspective"; the Three Distances are a *different, deliberate system* for organising a walked-through world (A/C). Present it as such, never as primitive pre-perspective.
5. **Calligraphy as texture.** See Module 03 §4.3: unreadable pseudo-strokes fail. Use real characters with real content, or use emptiness (留白) — which is itself the most authentic device available (D).

## 8. Gaps and limitations

- **Attribution debates:** several named works carry old attribution questions (e.g. some Guo Xi attributions; the Wang Ximeng scroll is unsigned and dated via Cai Jing's colophon) (A/C).[^11^]
- **Licensing granularity:** NPM Open Data images are CC BY 4.0 via the museum's open-data channel, while older scans circulating on Wikimedia Commons are tagged PD/PD-Art; the register records per-item status. Palace Museum Beijing does **not** operate a CC0 programme — the *Thousand Li* image used is a Commons PD-Art scan, not an official open-licensed asset.
- **Translations** of Guo Xi and other treatises quoted here are working translations assembled from the Chinese primary text as republished online; academic published translations (e.g. in Bush & Shih, *Early Chinese Texts on Painting*) were not directly consulted in this pass (limitation).
- **Han Zhuo's additional "distances"** and the 三絕 origin anecdote are included with explicit "unverified" flags.
- **Regional relevance:** Min/Fujian-specific painting traditions (e.g. 閩畫, temple mural practice relevant to Fuzhou lineage halls) are not covered here and deserve a dedicated module for the Qi'an Village material.
- PV-05's Commons image file is catalogued there under a Beijing Palace Museum version of the same composition; the Freer leaf differs in detail — see register note before reuse.
