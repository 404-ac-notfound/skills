# Module 02 — Calligraphy and Brushwork (書法五體與筆法)

**Library series:** Chinese art/design reference modules for an AI design assistant
**First application:** Chinese–English Malaysian Chinese family-history book, Qi/Chee (齊) family, Qi'an Village, Fuzhou
**Compiled:** 2026-11-07
**Claim labels:** (A) documented fact / primary statement · (B) visual observation · (C) scholarly interpretation · (D) proposed contemporary design application

---

## 1. Purpose

The family book will at some point touch calligraphy: a reproduced rubbing of an ancestral inscription, a calligraphed title, a brush-textured graphic. To use such material honestly, a designer needs (i) a working taxonomy of the five script families and their meaningful internal divisions, (ii) a vocabulary for brushwork qualities, and (iii) — most important — a clear rule about **what a finished work shows versus what it merely suggests**. This module supplies all three, anchored to canonical works with verifiable museum records.

## 2. The five script families (五體) and their real subdivisions

### 2.1 篆書 Seal script (A/C)

"Seal script" covers a longer span than any other family, and the subdivisions matter:

| Subdivision | Period & exemplar | Visible character (B/C) |
|---|---|---|
| **大篆** (greater seal), incl. 金文 bronze inscriptions and 籀文 | Western Zhou → pre-Qin. Exemplar: **石鼓文** (Stone Drum inscriptions), ten granite boulders now in the Palace Museum, Beijing; the earliest surviving stone inscriptions, each bearing a four-syllable hunting ode; dated by various scholars from Duke Wen to Duke Xiang of Qin down to Qin Shihuang's reign — **no scholarly consensus** on exact date.[^8^][^9^] | Squarish, dense, structurally irregular compared to Qin standardisation; strokes of even thickness with concealed tips (藏鋒); transitional between bronze script and small seal — "集大篆之成，開小篆之先河" (sums up greater seal, opens the way to small seal). |
| **小篆** (small seal) | Qin standardisation under 李斯 (traditionally), 221 BCE onward; later revived by Tang's 李陽冰 ("iron-wire" line, 鐵線篆) and by Qing masters (鄧石如, 吳讓之, 吳昌碩).[^10^] | Uniform, symmetrical, elongated rectangles; curves replace turns; mechanically even line width. |

Design note (D): seal script reads as *archaic authority* — appropriate for seal-carving motifs, a colophon ornament, or a family crest treatment; inappropriate for body text or any content a modern reader must actually parse.

### 2.2 隸書 Clerical script (A/C)

Born as a Qin-era speed-writing of seal script (秦隸), matured in the Eastern Han (漢隸) when it became the official script. Defining features (A/B): flattened, horizontally spreading structure ("八分" spread); the signature 蠶頭雁尾 — "silkworm head, swallow tail" — on principal horizontals; 一波三折 undulation; and the convention 雁不雙飛 (only one swallow-tail per character). Exemplar: **《曹全碑》** (Stele of Cao Quan), erected 185 CE, excavated at Heyang, Shaanxi in the early Wanli era, now in the Xi'an Beilin (Forest of Steles) Museum — the paradigm of the graceful (秀逸) pole of Han clerical script, with rounded yet tensile strokes; contrast the blunt, square 《張遷碑》 for the austere pole.[^7^][^11^]

*Data caution:* the Beilin Museum's own web page gives the stele as erected 186 CE (中平三年) and 272 × 95 cm, while the dominant scholarly and reference literature gives 185 CE (中平二年) and c. 253 × 123 cm. Treat 185 as the received date; the museum page discrepancy is noted in the exemplar register.[^7^][^11^]

### 2.3 楷書 Regular script (A/C)

Mature from Han-Wei (鍾繇) through the Tang, when it became the bureaucratic norm — hence 楷 = "model." The meaningful internal split:

| 魏碑 (Northern-dynasties stele style) | 唐楷 (Tang regular) |
|---|---|
| Northern Wei and successor-state stone inscriptions: 《張猛龍碑》, 《龍門二十品》, 《張玄墓誌》.[^12^] | 歐陽詢《九成宮醴泉銘》, 顏真卿《多寶塔碑》《顏勤禮碑》, 柳公權《玄秘塔碑》《神策軍碑》.[^12^] |
| Transitional, retaining clerical flavour; square-cut, angular strokes; asymmetrical, "dangerously balanced" structures; much of the perceived edge is **the carver's knife, not the writer's brush** — hence the teachers' warning 「透過刀鋒看筆鋒」 (see through the knife to the brush): do not imitate chisel damage with a brush.[^13^] | Codified, "rule-bound" (唐尚法): explicit discipline for entry, travel, and exit of every stroke; stable, centred structures; the standard training canon ever since.[^12^] |

Design note (D): 顏體 (Yan style) reads as upright, monumental, "loyal" — culturally the default register for commemorative and genealogical display; 魏碑 reads as archaic-rugged; 歐體 as refined-severe.

### 2.4 行書 Running script (A/C)

The semi-cursive middle register, "walking" between regular and cursive: 楷法多於草法 → 行楷; 草法多於楷法 → 行草.[^12^] It preserves legibility while admitting linking strokes (牽絲), fused components, and rhythmic variation. Canonical status markers: 王羲之《蘭亭序》 is traditionally called 天下第一行書; 顏真卿《祭姪文稿》 was praised by the Yuan connoisseur 鮮于樞 as 天下第二行書; 蘇軾《黃州寒食帖》 as third.[^2^][^4^][^12^] Running script is the natural register for a family book's calligraphic elements: dignified but personal. (D)

### 2.5 草書 Cursive script (A/C)

Not "messy writing" but a rule-governed abbreviation system (see Module 01, §4.2). The three historical strata:

| | 章草 | 今草 (小草) | 狂草 (大草) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Period | Han; clerical cursive regularised | Late Han (trad. 張芝) → Wei-Jin perfection (王羲之) | Tang (張旭, 懷素) |
| Rule (A) | Character-by-character, no inter-character linking; **retains clerical wave-strokes (波磔)**; flattened | Wave-strokes removed; linking between characters allowed; structure simplified by component substitution | Continuous "one-stroke" momentum across characters and even lines; extreme abbreviation |
| Exemplars (A) | 皇象《急就章》(松江本); 陸機《平復帖》 (earliest surviving ink letter) | 王羲之《十七帖》; 智永《真草千字文》; 孫過庭《書譜》 | 張旭《古詩四帖》《肚痛帖》; **懷素《自敘帖》** |
| Readability | Learnable | Trained readers only | Even contemporaries joked about illegibility (張丞相 story)[^14^] |

于右任's 20th-century《標準草書》 re-codified 今草 for modern use (Module 01, §4.2).[^15^]

## 3. Brushwork qualities: a working vocabulary

Each quality below is defined, then split into **what is visible (B)** versus **what is only inferable (C)**.

### 3.1 Pressure: 提按 (lifting and pressing)

The vertical dimension of brush control: press (按) spreads the hairs for a thick, dark stroke; lift (提) gathers them for a thin one.[^5^] **Visible:** the width and saturation envelope of every stroke; swelling entries, tapering exits; the thick–thin rhythm across a line (clearly visible in《祭姪文稿》, where pressure surges track the draft's agitation — see §5). (B) **Inferable:** the speed or emotion with which pressure changed; two different pressure *histories* can converge on similar shapes. (C)

### 3.2 Turning: 轉折

At a direction change the brush either **轉** (rounds — maintaining a centreline curve, typical of seal and of 顏真卿's rounded manner) or **折** (breaks — squaring the corner via a press and re-angle, typical of 歐陽詢 and of stele-square styles). **Visible:** corner geometry — rounded vs. angular. (B) **Inferable:** whether a rounded corner came from wrist rotation or from a slowed curve; and in *stone-carved* works, whether the knife or the brush owns the squareness (the 魏碑 caveat). (C)

### 3.3 Speed (疾澀 / 遲速)

**Visible:** side-effects only — streaking, edge character, ink-bleed behaviour, the spacing rhythm of dots. (B) **Inferable:** actual tempo. A slow, dragged brush and a fast dry brush can produce superficially similar "rough" textures; connoisseurship literature treats speed claims as reconstruction, not observation. (C)

### 3.4 Ink loading and taper (含墨量 / 收鋒)

A freshly loaded brush writes saturated and full; ink depletes along the stroke until the brush must be re-dipped. **Visible:** the "dip-cycle" — a sequence beginning dark and full and thinning to dry; in《自敘帖》scholars explicitly read re-dipping points off the scroll. (B/C)[^3^] **Inferable:** the size of the dip, or interruptions (a pause to think leaves no certain trace). (C)

### 3.5 Dry-brush / 飛白 (flying white)

When a fast or under-loaded brush splits, parallel streaks of white paper show inside the stroke — 飛白, literally "flying white," a term since Han times (trad. 蔡邕). **Visible:** unmistakable in original ink (e.g., streaked radicals in《自敘帖》and《祭姪文稿》). (B) **Critical caution (C):** 飛白 is *impossible to reproduce by tracing*: copyists and engravers fake it poorly, and connoisseurs use exactly this — the presence of natural, rhythmically consistent dry-brush effects — as an argument for authenticity (made explicitly in the《自敘帖》three-versions debate).[^3^] For designers: **don't imitate 飛白 with a texture filter for documentary material**; it reads as counterfeit to anyone who knows the feature. (D)

### 3.6 Stroke connection: 牽絲映帶 (linking threads) and abbreviation

Running and cursive scripts connect separate strokes with fine threads (牽絲) and merge/omit strokes along the writing path. **Visible:** which strokes physically touch; the contrast between thick structural strokes and hair-thin links. (B) **Inferable:** nothing about *intent* — but the connection pattern is rule-bound, not arbitrary: connections follow stroke order and cursive grammar (Module 01), which is why an invented "cursive-style" glyph betrays itself by connecting strokes that no writing path would join. (C)

### 3.7 Rhythm and spatial balance (節奏 / 章法 / 布白)

At composition scale: alternation of dense and open characters (疏密), of heavy and light ink masses (濃淡/虛實), column alignment and drift, and the management of negative space (計白當黑 — "treat the white as black"). **Visible:** all of it, as composition. (B) **Inferable:** claims that a specific spacing *expresses* a specific emotion — plausible in context (《祭姪文稿》), unfalsifiable in general. (C)

## 4. The hard epistemic rule: appearance vs. reconstruction

A finished rubbing or album leaf is a **static trace of a four-dimensional event** (3-D brush motion + time). Hence:

- **Cannot be reconstructed from appearance alone:** exact stroke order; the number and position of airborne pen-lifts; tempo and pauses; the artist's intent or emotional state. Anyone who claims to "read off" these with certainty is over-claiming. (C)
- **Can be observed directly:** stroke shapes, widths, saturation, connections, corrections (塗改), seal impressions, paper joins, column geometry. (B)
- **Can be *probabilistically inferred* with genre knowledge:** likely stroke order (against standard 筆順), likely re-dip points, whether a work is a traced copy (勾摹) — because tracing erases pressure gradients, kills natural 飛白, and freezes the dip-cycle. The 神龍本《蘭亭序》 is treasured precisely because it *mixes* tracing with free writing (摹臨結合), retaining "賊毫、叉筆" stray-hair accidents that pure tracing loses.[^6^] (B/C)

Design consequence (D): when reproducing calligraphy in the book, present it as *evidence* (facsimile, credited), never as a "cleaned-up" reconstruction; and never annotate a reproduction with invented stroke-order arrows as if recovered from the original.

## 7. Gaps and limitations

- **Attribution and authenticity are live debates.** The 神龍本's 神龍 seal is suspected to be a later addition (故宮's own 單國強 text says the 馮承素 attribution "也不可信"); the Taipei《自敘帖》 has a published three-versions authenticity controversy;《蘭亭序》's original is lost. This module uses these works as *tradition anchors*, not as settled autographs. (A/C)[^3^][^6^]
- **Object data conflicts exist even at institutions** (the Beilin 曹全碑 date/dimension discrepancy, §2.2). Register rows flag the specific conflicts found; none were resolved against physical catalogues raisonnés. (A)
- **"Emotion in the stroke" readings** (e.g., grief in《祭姪文稿》) are (C) interpretive traditions — culturally important, but not measurable claims; do not present them as (A).
- **Malaysian and diaspora calligraphic practice** (temple inscriptions around Fuzhou-lineage communities, Malaysian 書法 associations' conventions) is not covered by primary sources here; **(unverified)** and recommended as a later module.
- Digital images flatten ink depth and paper texture; all (B) observations are from reproductions, not from the objects.
- The NPM Open Data licence (CC BY 4.0) applies to the mid-tier (6 MP) images on the NPM/Commons files cited; always credit 「國立故宮博物院, CC BY 4.0」. Met images are CC0. Commons PD-art status follows the Wikimedia position on faithful 2-D reproductions, which some jurisdictions dispute. (A)
