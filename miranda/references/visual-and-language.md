# Visual and language judgment

## Read the object in sequence

1. **First encounter.** Read or view the actual export from beginning to end, using facing spreads, a slide sorter, the live page, or a timed preview as appropriate. Note the first thing the eye finds, where attention stalls, and whether the last beat pays off the opening. Use thumbnails to spot repeated emphasis, dead runs, and abrupt changes. Use the rendered proof to confirm them.
2. **Inspect the image.** At intended size, then close up, check subject, gesture, expression, direction of gaze, scale, foreground and background, light, edge crops, focal point, tonal separation, and visual truth. Check visible lettering for wrong characters or broken strokes and look for cloning, halos, or inconsistent reflections when relevant. Evaluate charts, photographs, and illustrations by what their audience will believe they show. Check provenance and caption against the source.
3. **Check the placement.** Locate the sentence, scene, page turn, slide, or moment the image serves. Glance across a facing spread before reading it. A prominent picture or caption can spoil a reveal anywhere on that spread. Test whether placement forces the reader to hunt for an explanation, duplicates the words, reverses the intended hierarchy, or breaks the grid. Compare crop and scale with adjacent images. Check the gutter and trim, overset type, caption proximity, contrast, text alternatives, and reading order where applicable. Use the actual output specifications or printer's profile.
4. **Read the seams.** Check the title against the opening, each transition against what precedes it, and the ending against the promise. Look for identical emotional register across unlike scenes, a stock image that drains specificity from a true account, narrative distance that changes mid-scene, a face cropped into unintended evasiveness, and a quiet spread overwhelmed by its neighbor. Give a location, the visible or textual cue, and the reader effect. An intuition is a lead for inspection.
5. **Test what the repair touches.** Mentally remove, move, or replace the disputed element and run through the surrounding material again. A strong portrait on the wrong spread can be a sequencing problem. A beautiful crop that changes a chart's meaning fails the evidence check. A deliberate pause or irregular image size can earn its place. Check the next proof as a whole before clearing a consequential change.

Render or open the asset yourself when the venue permits. Use the actual version under review. For a PDF, cite the printed folio and PDF index when they differ. If you can read extracted text but cannot see the embedded art, locate an existing proof or export the affected pages with the tools available. If you cannot get the visual evidence, name the pages or spreads you need. Keep the layout decision open. [W3C image alternatives](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/images/), [Adobe print preflight](https://helpx.adobe.com/indesign/desktop/print/preflight/configure-and-use-the-preflight-panel.html).

## Choose references by language, audience, and purpose

Project style sheet, approved spellings and translations, genre, and delivery locale govern choice of reference. Use a style authority to resolve a convention and original published work to calibrate craft. Read each language on its own for idiom, register, and cadence, then compare parallel passages for changes in claim, emphasis, agency, and omissions. Verify names and romanization against the project's records or the person's documented usage. Inspect titles, captions, folios, and credits in the rendered proof. Preserve deliberate dialect and historical forms. Use an in-language authority or a qualified reader when the text's publication depends on a convention you cannot verify.

| Job | What to consult |
| --- | --- |
| English fiction or memoir | Use the project style sheet first. Check point of view, tense, timeline, character names, and deliberate diction using the [Chartered Institute of Editing and Proofreading's fiction style sheet guidance](https://www.ciep.uk/resource/fiction-style-sheets.html). Use the [English chapter examples](benchmark-library.md#chapters) for voice, scene pressure, and endings. |
| English reporting or service copy | Follow house style and the audience's English variety. Use [AP's sourcing and attribution standards](https://www.ap.org/about/news-values-and-principles/telling-the-story/) for reporting, [GOV.UK clear structure](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/clear-structure/) for public-service hierarchy, or the [Australian Government Style Manual on word choice](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/writing-and-designing-content/clear-language-and-writing-style/plain-language-and-word-choice) for plain-language checks. Verify a disputed UK or US usage in [Cambridge English Grammar Today](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/grammar/british-grammar/british-and-american-english). |
| Chinese prose and publishing | Establish locale, script, register, and horizontal or vertical typesetting. For Mainland Chinese punctuation use [GB/T 15834](https://openstd.samr.gov.cn/bzgk/std/newGbInfo?hcno=22EA6D162E4110E752259661E1A0D0A8). For Taiwan use the Ministry of Education's [punctuation handbook](https://language.moe.gov.tw/001/upload/files/site_content/m0001/hau/haushou.htm) and [國語辭典簡編本](https://dict.concised.moe.edu.tw/) for everyday usage. Use Hong Kong or Malaysian Chinese house usage and locally published sources where relevant. [W3C Chinese Text Layout](https://www.w3.org/TR/clreq/) covers regional composition, punctuation and line-breaking differences. |
| Chinese calligraphy or Chinese–English design | Locate the approved text and any glyph, script, seal, or family-form decision. Read the relevant character, brushwork, composition, or bilingual typography module and the evaluation sheet in [Chinese Design Reference](https://github.com/404-ac-notfound/skills/tree/e4d23fcac55a324bcc9f90fee16409deade98156/chinese-design-reference). For a composed calligraphic asset, compare the documented style and glyph sources with the relevant [Wang Shi Fu references](https://github.com/404-ac-notfound/skills/tree/daab10950fbe5deb4f2e31ae3538652ba8004bfe/wsf/references). Review the actual glyph sequence, structure, joins, spacing, scale and placement. Require attestation or a recorded project exception for a nonstandard form. Distinguish visual evidence from claims about stroke order, authorship, or intent. |
| Malaysian Malay | Use the house style for voice and established names. Check spelling and specialist vocabulary against [Dewan Bahasa dan Pustaka's language guides](https://dbp.gov.my/pedoman-lembar-bahasa/) and [PRPM](https://prpm.dbp.gov.my/). In a multilingual asset, check the Malay layout and idiom directly. |
| Other languages | Locate the project's style authority and published exemplars in the same language, market, and genre. Use translation to check meaning where helpful. Make a language-quality call only to the extent the evidence and the reader's competence support it. |

The Chinese companion links point to pinned revisions on separate pull requests. Their documentation lists some image assets that are absent from Git. Use linked canonical collection records for visual inspection and the recorded source rights for reproduction. If the companion files are unavailable in the venue, use the relevant external sources and inspect the actual print or image proof. Taiwan's Ministry of Education [Dictionary of Chinese Character Variants](https://dict.variants.moe.edu.tw/) helps verify variant structure. Use a traceable calligraphic example to judge a specific handwritten form. An unverified glyph or credit stays open.

## Compare actual work

- **English illustrated books.** Open *The Snowy Day* in the publisher's preview. Follow Peter across the snow and read what the page lets the marks and space show. For an illustrated chapter, find the beat the image makes available to the reader and test whether it arrives on the right side of the turn. [Ezra Jack Keats, publisher preview](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/315149/the-snowy-day-by-ezra-jack-keats/).
- **English typography and image density.** Look at the opening of the 1896 *Kelmscott Chaucer*. The illustrations, dense borders, decorated initials, and blocks of type operate at different levels. On a decorative commission, inspect where the density directs attention and where the actual reading begins. [Digitized book, Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/item/02018643/), [collection guide](https://guides.loc.gov/medievalism/morris).
- **Chinese literary prose.** In Lu Xun's 《故鄉》, compare the remembered young 閏土 with the encounter when he calls the narrator 老爺. A tiny change of address carries an entire social distance. Check dialogue address and how much exposition the surrounding scene needs once the register does that work. Compare the end's road image with the last pages of the draft when an abstract ending needs a physical consequence. [Original Chinese text, Wikisource](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/%E6%95%85%E9%84%89).
- **Chinese calligraphy.** Inspect the three letters attributed to Wang Xizhi in the National Palace Museum's record. Compare stroke entry and exit, joins, and spacing at both word and column scale. The record identifies the object as a copy made by outlining and filling, which matters when describing what the visible strokes prove. [National Palace Museum digital archive](https://digitalarchive.npm.gov.tw/Collection/Detail?dep=P&id=1).

For shorter forms, compare the pacing, hierarchy, and image-text relationship in the [benchmark library](benchmark-library.md). Use one near comparator when possible. Borrow the choice that improves the reader's experience and preserve the asset's own design language.
