# Venue and verification

Apply the same bar in every venue. Judge the draft against what its audience came to read, understand, decide, or do.

| Format | Inspect | Return on REVISE |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Article or essay | Make the lead and headline promise the same piece. Put the governing idea where the reader needs it. Check the chain from evidence to conclusion, the strongest relevant contradiction, and the consequence at the end. | Working headline and complete edited copy, with supported attribution where it belongs. Keep reporting requests outside the copy. |
| Chapter | Name what changes by the end. Track point of view, chronology, geography, motive, and scene order. Preserve intentional silence, cadence, and character diction. Know which question the chapter answers and which one it leaves alive. | Complete edited chapter or clearly scoped excerpt when the supplied material or response size limits coverage. |
| Post | Check the first visible line, what survives truncation, the combined job of copy and media, and what the reader can actually do. Check destination, timing, accessibility, and platform limits when they affect delivery. | Final copy plus any integral link, caption, image copy, or text alternative the available information supports. |
| Deck | Pick a primary mode: spoken presentation or read-ahead. Read the slide titles in sequence. Make each slide advance one audience question. Check chart scales, axes, units, labels, and sources. End with the decision or next move and the terms already established in the draft. | Slide-by-slide replacement with on-slide copy, visual directions, and notes only where they carry needed context. Evaluate the rendered layout if supplied. |
| Image brief | State what the viewer must notice first and why. Check subject, gesture, setting, light, crop, type placement, whitespace, credits, and rights when relevant. Assign separate jobs to headline, caption, and text alternative. | A production-ready brief with one concept, concrete art direction, known delivery requirements, and any unresolved dependencies recorded outside the brief. Judge a draft image only if supplied and visible. |

For other formats, carry over the closest venue's checks and apply the actual constraints of the destination. For a brief, assess readiness for the next production step. For a finished asset, assess readiness for the audience.

## When the evidence is incomplete

- Match a source to the exact claim. Read the original where possible. For a figure, check the denominator, population, units, timeframe, version, and calculation. For a quote, check wording, speaker, context, and attribution. For a visual, check what it depicts and whether it can be used in this context.
- Internally distinguish what is supported, what needs a source, what conflicts with evidence, and what is explicitly opinion or inference. In the edit, surface a gap at its location with the evidence needed to close it. For example, `Slide 4 — Needs source: 24% of which customers, over what period?`
- Remove an unsupported claim when it adds nothing essential. Narrow it only to wording the evidence actually supports. A missing source for a material claim in the submitted draft blocks PUBLISH. If the claim is essential, hold the revised piece pending that source. If removing it collapses the premise and makes the assignment untenable, KILL.
- If sources or media are inaccessible, say which verification remains open. Assess the material you can see. An estimate, forecast, comparison, or reported statement should keep its qualifying language. Give open issues in order of consequence in the notes.

For sourcing, attribution, and corrections, consult [Associated Press, Telling the Story](https://www.ap.org/about/news-values-and-principles/telling-the-story/). For public-service hierarchy, see [GOV.UK, Create a clear structure for your content](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/clear-structure/). For text alternatives, use [W3C WAI, Images Tutorial](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/images/).
