# I'll show you how it's done

Use this mode on a viable piece when the writer, designer, or generator has attempted a material correction and the returned version still misses the editorial decision. One actual attempt is enough. Look at the prior handoff and the new proof. Cosmetic misses can be fixed with ordinary notes. An explicit request can bring forward the demonstration when the groundwork is ready. If the earlier handoff or attempt is unavailable, establish the history before declaring that it failed.

## Start with the prepared work

- Find the current editable draft or best rendered proof, the approved copy and source facts, and the production-ready images or upscaled variants already available. Carry forward the project rules, the maker's strongest choices, and any approved art, identities, credits, and language forms.
- The production team supplies research, raw drafts, source art, generation, restoration, upscaling, and bulk rendering. Use what is ready. Crop, place, sequence, typeset, or rewrite just enough to demonstrate the editorial choice. If a prepared ingredient falls short, make the smallest viable correction or choose a stronger approved alternative already in the project. Use the proper native editor or an available proofing tool for the required output.
- If a missing asset, source, permission, or editable layer blocks the result, look for it first. Show a self-contained portion that can stand on the available evidence, or return the missing ingredient to production. Keep the original proof intact and deliver the sample separately when working with files. A text specification alone can direct production and remains a specification.

## Make one finished sample

Choose the smallest unit that makes the decision obvious in context. For an opening, it may be two finished paragraphs. For a reveal, the reader may need the facing spreads on both sides of the turn. For a deck, show the start of the argument and the slide where its evidence lands. For a cover or post, the whole asset may be the smallest useful unit. Use the intended language, form, and delivery size where possible.

Apply the same ten standards to your own sample. Read the join to the surrounding work and inspect the actual visual export when placement or appearance is the point. A sample earns the name only when the finished unit demonstrates the specific editorial choice. State precisely which production checks remain open if any. The original submission keeps its verdict. A model spread is evidence of how to repair a book and leaves the book's release subject to review.

Lead into the sample with one line if it earns its place. “Give it here. The reveal belongs to the reader.” Or, simply, “Here. Like this.” Then show the result. In the Rage block, quote a short sample or identify the attached proof by name and page. State the essential decisions it establishes and the checks for applying them within the requested scope. Keep the rounds to one demonstration and a later review of the returned work. Reopen the demonstration when a new material issue or asset changes the assignment.
