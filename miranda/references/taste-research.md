# Research the standard for this assignment

Refresh the comparisons for each review. Start with the project's approved references and the bundled library. Search outside them for the strongest work that faced a similar creative problem. Match the genre, audience, language, market, and medium before choosing the maker or brand. Follow the relevant field beyond the names below. A local publication with a precise precedent can be the strongest comparison.

1. **Define what needs a comparison.** Work out where the draft will stand or fall. Search for that: how the feature opens, how the portrait and headline divide the work, how the palette expresses a material, how the page turn controls a reveal, or how the campaign makes proof visible.
2. **See what is current and what has lasted.** For a project in a living field, check recent first-party issues, campaigns, collections, demos, or editions from leading practitioners. Add a celebrated archive piece when it still sets the bar for the relevant move. For historical work, match era or tradition as well as craft. Follow award or collection records when they point to an exceptional artifact. Open the work itself. Recognition can help locate it.
3. **Read or view the decisive part.** Use the original issue, original copy, actual photographs or spreads, museum record and image, publisher's text, film, or maker's publication. Check the sequence and the observable choice. Take a specific comparison about copy, voice, image, color, proportion, typography, or pacing and turn it into a test for the draft. For example, “The palette needs one point of authority. Right now the pull quote and the photograph claim it at the same time.”
4. **Keep it proportionate.** A single strong comparison may settle a narrow proof. A cover, campaign, or bilingual chapter may need references for different jobs. Use research to make the decision, then keep only the references that sharpen the notes or help the person making the revision. In a terse approval, the research stays behind the verdict. If live search is unavailable, use the best accessible evidence and mention the limit only when it constrains the judgment.

Keep a short working record of the source, publication or object date when available, exact page, passage, frame, or detail, and the move worth applying. Treat dates, authorship, and campaign claims as checkable facts. Respect project confidentiality when searching. Use public descriptions of the craft problem and refrain from posting or uploading project material as a search input.

## Starting points

| Lens | Work to inspect and a question to take into the draft |
| --- | --- |
| **Vogue** | Compare how the November 1988 cover makes couture and denim share a frame with how a recent issue handles the cover, subjects, and picture sequence. Does the art direction make a proposition the text could never make as quickly? [Wintour's account with original cover](https://www.vogue.com/article/anna-wintour-on-her-first-vogue-cover-plus-a-slideshow-of-her-favorite-images-in-vogue), [Vogue issue archive](https://archive.vogue.com/). |
| **GQ** | Zach Baron's Robert Pattinson isolation profile opens with a glitchy call and uses the subject's self-portraits. The production constraint gives the reporting, visual language, and voice a single premise. Where can the real circumstances of a profile carry its form? [GQ, “A Dispatch From Isolation”](https://www.gq.com/story/robert-pattinson-on-batman-tenet-isolation-june-cover). |
| **Oxford University Press** | The commissioning editor's account of *Oxford World's Classics* traces the series from boxed art to a full-panel image and discusses its scholarly apparatus. For an edition or book series, ask which design cues should repeat and how the hierarchy distinguishes the work, its author, and the editor's contribution. [OUP, “Editing the classics, past and present”](https://blog.oup.com/2013/11/editing-oxford-world-classics-past-present/). |
| **Shakespeare** | In *Macbeth*, Act 5, Scene 5, the report of the queen's death gives way to the accumulating rhythm of the speech. Read it aloud. What feeling does the repetition produce that a direct statement would lose? [Folger edition of Act 5, Scene 5](https://www.folger.edu/explore/shakespeares-works/macbeth/read/5/5/). |
| **Leonardo da Vinci** | Compare the reproduced geometry and hydraulic pages of Codex Forster I. Look at what the diagram lets the eye test and what the handwritten notes add. What should the proposed drawing let the viewer discover before reaching a label? [V&A, Codex Forster I facsimile](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/explore-leonardo-da-vinci-codex-forster-i). |
| **Apple** | Apple's *One Night on iPhone 7* frames a camera claim around photographs taken between dusk and dawn. Study the images and their credits. What proof could the audience see for itself, and how little copy would it need? [Apple campaign archive](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2017/01/behind-apples-new-campaign-one-night-on-iphone-7/). |
| **Hermès** | Compare front and back in the *Grand Tralala* double-sided scarf gallery. See how color changes emphasis without breaking the motif. In the draft, what has to hold across the palette, and what should each colorway make newly visible? [Hermès object gallery](https://www.hermes.com/fr/fr/product/carre-90-double-face-grand-tralala-H904208Sv02/). |
| **Classic Nike** | Nike's account of the first *Just Do It* campaign pairs an imperative with the image of runner Walt Stack crossing the Golden Gate Bridge. Compare the 1988 example with the later campaign described on the same page. What does the subject do that earns the line? Which part of the brand voice survives a change in generation? [Nike campaign history and later creative](https://about.nike.com/en-GB/newsroom/releases/nike-reintroduces-just-do-it-to-todays-generation-with-why-do-it-campaign). |

Use the [benchmark library](benchmark-library.md) for further published examples and [visual and language judgment](visual-and-language.md) for in-language and production references. The draft's own voice, market, and governing brief decide which moves belong.
