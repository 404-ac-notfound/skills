# Benchmark library

Use the entry closest to the draft's job. Read the passage, slide, or visual named in the entry. Identify the craft choice, the work it performs for its audience, and the equivalent choice the draft could make in its own voice. The ratings apply to the move under study. They are editorial judgments about execution, independent of sales, awards, or reputation.

**Good** delivers the expected job. **Great** anticipates the reader's next question and makes the answer feel deliberate. **Amazing** makes a precise, distinctive choice central to how the work lands. Great is where the final edit starts. Amazing is the publication bar.

## Articles and essays

### Good — Office for National Statistics, “What is inflation and how do we calculate it?”

The opening defines the measure, sets a year-over-year comparison, and signals why households can experience different price changes. The title makes an honest promise that the body fulfills. In a service explainer, give the direct answer and its scope before the method. Read the text above “How do we calculate inflation?” [ONS explainer](https://www.ons.gov.uk/aboutus/usingpublicdatatoproducestatistics/learningaboutdata/learnmoreaboutwhoweareandwhatwedo/whatisinflationandhowdowecalculateit).

### Great — Cloudflare, “Cloudflare outage on November 18, 2025”

The first screen gives readers the impact, a plain account of what triggered it, and recovery status. The body then carries the technical depth and a timeline. Study the change in level of detail. Use this order when affected readers need an immediate answer and specialists need the full mechanism. Check that labels for system onset, customer impact, and recovery stay distinct throughout. [Cloudflare's incident account](https://blog.cloudflare.com/18-november-2025-outage/).

### Amazing — Annie Dillard, “Total Eclipse”

The essay makes the mind's struggle to name the event part of the event itself. Study the hotel clown portrait, the diner where a stranger reaches for an ordinary candy comparison, and the last paragraph as the watchers hurry home. The ordinary details have a structural job. They give the reader a way into and out of the strange experience. In a personal essay, find the particular detail that can change meaning as the piece moves. Keep it if it carries that turn. [The Atlantic's authorized excerpt](https://www.theatlantic.com/science/archive/2017/08/annie-dillards-total-eclipse/536148/).

For reported multimedia, read the Times' submission for John Branch's *Snow Fall*, reproduced on the Pulitzer site. Its account of Branch's reporting and the resulting presentation is a prompt to give interactive media a job in the telling. Ask what the reader understands through the medium that prose alone would struggle to deliver. [Pulitzer archive and original entry link](https://www.pulitzer.org/winners/john-branch).

## Chapters

### Good — Robert Louis Stevenson, *Treasure Island*, Chapter I

The narrator gives himself a reason to tell the story, withholds the island's bearings because treasure remains, and takes the reader to the inn and the scarred seaman in the same sentence. The frame earns its place through the withheld detail and the immediate arrival. Give necessary orientation a live question to carry. Read the first paragraph of “The Old Sea-dog at the ‘Admiral Benbow.’” [Project Gutenberg text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/120/120-h/120-h.htm).

### Great — Jane Austen, *Pride and Prejudice*, Chapter I

The mock-universal opening is put to work immediately in the Bennets' conversation about Netherfield. Mrs. Bennet wants a visit arranged. Mr. Bennet delays by making her spell out why. The chapter's final portrait makes the marriage agenda unmistakable. Let a conversation reveal the social rule through two people wanting different things from the exchange. [Project Gutenberg text, Chapter I](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1342/1342-h/1342-h).

### Amazing — N. K. Jemisin, *The Fifth Season*, prologue

The opening makes the end of the world both a continent-scale event and a mother's private catastrophe. The narrator's conversational invitation makes the scale shift more unsettling. The two meanings give the epic premise an intimate measure. When a chapter must introduce a large world, find the personal stakes that make the scale intelligible and put both on the page early. Read the opening excerpt under “prologue: you are here.” [Publisher-authorized excerpt and interview, PBS NewsHour](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/arts/sometimes-readers-need-to-be-traumatized-author-n-k-jemisin-says), [Hachette edition and excerpt link](https://www.littlebrown.co.uk/titles/n-k-jemisin/the-fifth-season/9780356508191/).

## Posts

### Good — GitHub Changelog, “Footnotes now supported in Markdown fields”

The title delivers the change. The body shows where it applies and points to the documentation. For a product update, give the reader the change, the affected surface, and a direct route to use it. Read the whole post for its economy. [GitHub Changelog](https://github.blog/changelog/2021-09-30-footnotes-now-supported-in-markdown-fields/).

### Great — Office for National Statistics, “Inflation: How it’s reported and its impact on prices”

The post turns a percentage into a £100-to-£108 example, then uses a reported change in rates to explain why prices can still be rising. The contrast is the post's reason to exist. Find the inference readers are most likely to get wrong and give them a worked example they can reuse. Study the first five paragraphs after the introduction. [ONS National Statistical blog](https://blog.ons.gov.uk/2022/12/14/inflation-how-its-reported-and-its-impact-on-prices/).

### Amazing — Oreo and 360i, Super Bowl blackout post

The caption acknowledges the moment. A single cookie, a dark frame, and the line on the image supply a response only that product could make so naturally. Copy and image do distinct work and land together in one glance. For a reactive post, check whether the moment, subject, and visual idea depend on one another. If another brand's logo could go on it unchanged, rewrite the idea. [Original creative in the Clio archive](https://www.adsoftheworld.com/campaigns/super-bowl-dunk-in-the-dark), [Team member Leo Morejon's account](https://leonardom.com/case-studies/oreo-super-bowl-blackout-tweet).

## Decks and presentations

### Good — Buffer, seed pitch deck

The “Traction” slide puts paying users and annualized revenue run rate where the investor can see them immediately. The unlabeled line shows the limit of the slide as a model. Its shape cannot establish a period or magnitude on its own. Put the most useful evidence on the slide and give any chart the scale, dates, and definitions it needs to be read. [Deck as shared by Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/the-slide-deck-we-used-to-raise-half-a-million-dollars/), [Original slides, PDF mirror](https://pitchmagic.ai/library/buffer/buffer-pitch-deck.pdf).

### Great — Reid Hoffman, LinkedIn Series B deck and annotations

Slides 5–9 use familiar network businesses to make an unfamiliar model legible, then bring that logic back to LinkedIn. The analogies clarify the mechanism. The business still needs its own evidence. Read Hoffman's notes on the last two slides as well. He would put the investment thesis on the final screen during questions. End an audience decision deck with the case for the decision still in view. [Reid Hoffman's slides and annotations](https://www.reidhoffman.org/linkedin-pitch-to-greylock/).

### Amazing — Steve Jobs, Macworld 2007 iPhone introduction

The opening presents three familiar devices, repeats the sequence, then reveals that they are one. The audience experiences the premise before Jobs names the product. This is repetition that earns its time. In a live deck, make the audience's understanding turn at a specific beat. Test that the sequence still reads clearly without the speaker if the deck must also travel on its own. See “Speech at Macworld.” [Steve Jobs Archive transcript](https://book.stevejobsarchive.com/), [Apple launch announcement](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2007/01/09Apple-Reinvents-the-Phone-with-iPhone/).

## Image briefs and visual direction

These are published results. Use them to infer a brief that could produce the same kind of effect.

### Good — NASA, Webb’s First Deep Field

The first-image gallery lets the image supply detail and uses the accompanying copy to identify the cluster, convey scale through a grain-of-sand comparison, and credit the contributors. Brief the image to supply the visual evidence. Give the caption the context the pixels cannot convey. See the first image and its accompanying entry. [NASA Science gallery](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/webbs-first-images/).

### Great — Paula Scher and Pentagram, The Public Theater

Study the posters in the monograph, including *Bring in ’da Noise, Bring in ’da Funk*. Street-derived typography is part of the theater's voice. Variations make individual productions distinct within a recognizable identity. Brief which design elements carry the institution and where the production can take over. Restraint means control of the hierarchy, even in a loud poster. [Pentagram's portfolio and project account](https://www.pentagram.com/work/25-years-at-the-public-a-love-story/story).

### Amazing — Anna Wintour’s first American *Vogue* cover, November 1988

Michaela Bercu wears a jeweled Lacroix jacket and Guess jeans in Peter Lindbergh's street photograph. The wider framing lets the clothes make the editorial argument, and the wind and off-camera gaze keep the picture alive. The cover makes couture feel at home in daily life in a single frame. Write the visual proposition in one sentence. Pick the crop and gesture that make it readable without explanation. [The cover and Wintour's account in Vogue](https://www.vogue.com/article/anna-wintour-on-her-first-vogue-cover-plus-a-slideshow-of-her-favorite-images-in-vogue).

### Amazing — Veruschka photographed by Richard Avedon for Vreeland-era *Vogue*, March 1967

Compare the spreads on bouncing skirts, sparkle, and black lace. Veruschka's movement makes the clothes' behavior visible. The recurring approach binds the sequence while each spread demonstrates a different quality. In a multi-image brief, give the subject something to do that reveals what matters, then give each selected frame a new job. [Vogue's archive reprint with spreads and captions](https://www.vogue.com/article/25-pages-of-veruschka-photographed-by-avedon-for-vogue-in-1967).

## Editorial practice

- **Choose the structure before polishing the prose.** McPhee describes grouping material around a lead, choosing an ending before going back to the beginning, and beginning a travel story with a later episode that does more for the reader than Day 1. Sketch the sequence around the opening promise and payoff. Keep chronology when it serves that sequence. [John McPhee, “Structure,” The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2013/01/14/structure).
- **Keep the evidence attached to the claim.** AP's standards address attribution of disputed facts, faithful quotation, the integrity of visuals, and visible corrections. When a draft sounds more certain than the evidence, repair the claim before repairing the sentence. [AP, Telling the Story](https://www.ap.org/about/news-values-and-principles/telling-the-story/).
